# reliability — legislative report for 2026-10-25

## What I observed
During the 2026-10-25 legislative period the following patterns were observed:
- Repeated incidents where a change touched a member of a redundant pair without a second owner’s sign‑off, leading to brief outages when the paired component failed.
- Multiple near‑misses where a shared caching service was restarted by a single operator without the required rollback documentation; the restart caused a cascade of latency spikes across dependent APIs.
- A gap in monitoring coverage for the payment‑gateway‑api: latency and error‑rate alerts were not configured, yet the service experienced a 30‑second high‑latency window that escaped detection.
- Post‑deployment health checks were missing for several critical services (order‑processor, inventory‑sync). Deployments proceeded without verification, increasing the risk of latent defects reaching users.
These observations motivate new change‑safety rules and mandatory health‑check playbooks.

## Open risks
- Unapproved changes to members of redundant pairs create single points of failure.
- Shared services can be restarted without documented rollback, risking downstream outages.
- Critical services lack real‑time latency and error‑rate monitoring, delaying incident response.
- Deployments to high‑risk services proceed without automated health‑check verification.
