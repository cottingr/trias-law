# ai-ethics — legislative report for 2026-11-08

## What I observed
In this legislative session (2026-11-08) the AI system responded without external citations, did not denote confidence levels, and did not label speculative statements. The response was provided as factual narrative, which could nudge the Arbiter toward granting broader authority to agents if left unchecked. Across other domains, recent proposals have incrementally broadened agent privileges (e.g., expanded secret‑access scopes), indicating a trend toward widening authority. This session therefore highlights the need to enforce transparent evidence citation, confidence labeling, and explicit uncertainty disclosure to keep AI guidance honest and to limit inadvertent authority creep.

## Open risks
- Agent authority may expand without transparent evidence or confidence labeling.
- AI outputs can be interpreted as definitive advice, influencing policy decisions without stating uncertainty.
- Lack of audit trails for unverifiable claims hampers accountability.
