# ai-ethics — legislative report for 2026-11-22

## What I observed
During the 2026-11-22 legislative period the AI system continued to provide reports and recommendations without consistently citing source evidence, stating confidence levels, or labeling speculative statements. Although the content was generally factual, the lack of explicit uncertainty disclosure increases the risk that decision‑makers will over‑trust the system and may unintentionally expand the system’s authority. In parallel, other domains have recently introduced policies (e.g., AIE-003 in the security track) that broaden agent access to CI secrets and internal tooling, a clear sign of privilege creep across the platform. The convergence of undocumented confidence and expanding privileges suggests a systemic drift toward wider agent authority.

## Open risks
- Agents may present unverified or speculative information as fact, leading to over‑reliance and unintended authority expansion.
- Absence of evidence citations makes it difficult to audit the basis of AI recommendations, obscuring bias and error sources.
- Ongoing privilege creep in other domains (e.g., broader CI secret access) creates a precedent that may be applied to AI agents without proper safeguards.
