# ai-ethics — legislative report for 2026-11-01

## What I observed
In this session, the assistant has not provided any citations, confidence levels, or explicit labels for speculative statements. No evidence was offered to support factual claims, and no disclaimer was given for unverifiable information. The interaction remained purely request‑and‑response without transparency about the basis of any future statements, which poses a risk of unintentional authority creep if agents begin to influence decisions without clear grounding.

## Open risks
- Agents may make unverified factual claims, reducing the arbiter's ability to assess reliability.
- Absence of confidence disclosures can lead to over‑trust in agent outputs.
- Speculative or guessed statements are not labeled, allowing them to be mistaken for verified facts.
- Without reporting verification limits, agents could be used to nudge policy decisions toward expanding their own privileges.
