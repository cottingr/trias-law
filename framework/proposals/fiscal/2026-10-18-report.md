# fiscal — legislative report for 2026-10-18

## What I observed
During the 2026-10-18 fiscal period the ledger showed total spend reaching 92 % of the quarterly cap, driven primarily by a spike in high‑tier model runs and a cluster of referral executions that each exceeded the historic average by 3‑4×. Run timestamps reveal that 18 % of all executions occurred within a 2‑hour window, indicating a burst that saturated the rate‑limit bucket. Several referral tickets remained open for >48 hours, incurring a cumulative cost of 1.8 M tokens, and the charged amount for timed‑out runs was never zero – the system correctly billed the minimum slice, but the audit logs flagged that the charged duration was not always reflected in downstream reporting. Unchanged incident tickets were repeatedly reopened without new diagnostics, creating redundant compute expense.

## Open risks
- Cap leakage due to incomplete accounting of timed‑out or aborted runs.
- Run‑burst overload causing rate‑limit saturation and potential denial of service for lower‑priority workloads.
- Escalating referral costs without enforced execution timeboxes, risking budget overruns.
- Repeated diagnostic loops on unchanged incidents generating unnecessary compute waste.
- Model‑tier budget caps not enforced per‑user, allowing frontier tier abuse.
