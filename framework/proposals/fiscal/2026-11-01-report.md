# fiscal — legislative report for 2026-11-01

## What I observed
During the 2026-11-01 period the Ledger shows three noteworthy patterns. First, total spend approached the provisional 10 M‑USD cap on three consecutive days, with the daily delta dropping below 2 % of the cap before rebounding, indicating that the current soft‑limit is being stretched. Second, run submissions clustered in 10‑minute bursts during business‑hour peaks, creating short‑term spikes that exceeded the average QPS threshold by 150 % and caused queue‑bottlenecks. Third, referrals to the advisory tier frequently exceeded the 30‑minute target, averaging 48 minutes and incurring an extra 0.12 USD per minute, while timed‑out runs were still billed at the full minute granularity (no zero‑charge anomalies). These trends reveal systemic pressure points that merit immediate policy action.

## Open risks
- Budget cap overrun risk – daily spend hovering within 2 % of the 10 M‑USD limit could trigger uncontrolled overspend if demand spikes persist.
- Run‑rate congestion – bursty submissions overload the scheduler, leading to increased latency and higher per‑run cost due to extended runtime.
- Referral latency inflation – advisory referrals regularly exceed the 30‑minute SLA, inflating costs and delaying decision cycles.
- Cost‑honesty leakage – while timed‑out runs are charged, the lack of a hard floor creates a minor revenue leak that could be abused.
