# security — legislative report for 2026-10-18

## What I observed
During the period 2026-10-18 the monitoring ledger recorded several privilege‑drift incidents:
1. The "dev‑full" IAM role granted developers read/write access to all S3 buckets, including production data stores, while the job functions only require read‑only access to a single analytics bucket.
2. CI pipelines executed under a shared service account were able to retrieve *any* secret from the secret manager; the pipelines only declared usage of two database passwords.
3. An automated health‑check script was granted blanket network egress to the entire VPC, yet its purpose is limited to probing a single health‑check endpoint.
4. The master encryption key identifier was logged in plaintext to CloudWatch Logs by a diagnostic tool, exposing the secret reference outside its intended secret‑namespace policy.
These findings indicate that access scopes, allow‑lists, and secret handling are broader than the principle of least privilege demands.

## Open risks
- Over‑privileged developer IAM role enables accidental or malicious modification of production data.
- CI pipelines can exfiltrate any secret, increasing the blast radius of a compromised pipeline.
- Unrestricted VPC egress for health‑check scripts expands the attack surface for lateral movement.
- Encryption key leakage in logs creates a secret‑exposure vector that can be harvested by an attacker.
